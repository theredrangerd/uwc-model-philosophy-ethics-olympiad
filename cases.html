⚖️ Philosophical Framework Matrix

Six foundational traditions tested in match deliberations
⚡ Consequentialism

Utilitarianism

Bentham • Mill • Singer

An action is morally right if it produces the greatest net happiness (utility) for the greatest number of sentient beings.

Key Distinction: Act vs. Rule Utility; Mill's qualitative higher pleasures of the intellect.
📜 Duty Ethics

Kantian Deontology

Immanuel Kant • Korsgaard

Actions are intrinsically right or wrong regardless of outcomes, evaluated by duty and the Categorical Imperative.

Core Maxim: Never treat human beings merely as means to an end, but always as ends in themselves.
🏛️ Character & Eudaimonia

Virtue Ethics

Aristotle • MacIntyre • Nussbaum

Cultivating excellent character traits (virtues) through practical wisdom (*Phronesis*) aiming for human flourishing (*Eudaimonia*).

The Golden Mean: Virtue is the deliberate balance between deficiency and excess.
🤝 Relational Theory

Ethics of Care

Gilligan • Held • Noddings

Moral duties arise from authentic human relationships, empathy, mutual vulnerability, and the preservation of trust.

Application: Highlights invisible caregiving labor and focuses on repairing relational harm.
⚖️ Social Contract

Rawlsian Justice

John Rawls • Scanlon

Fair terms of social cooperation are those that rational, equal persons choose from behind a *Veil of Ignorance*.

Difference Principle: Inequalities are permissible only if they benefit the least advantaged.
🧬 Applied Bioethics

Principlism (4 Pillars)

Beauchamp & Childress

The four universal bioethical pillars applied by clinical ethics boards and public health regulators globally.

The 4 Pillars: Autonomy, Beneficence, Non-Maleficence, and Distributive Justice.
